# Hero banner — image generation prompt

Source prompt for `assets/img/banner.jpg`, the full-bleed hero band on the landing page.

**Prompt:**

A wide horizontal banner image with a 4:1 aspect ratio — 2400 pixels wide by 600 pixels tall — composed as a website hero band, with the subject spread evenly across the full width.

An editorial illustration about learning, exchange and artificial intelligence, in the style of a modern magazine cover feature. Layered paper-cut collage with real dimensionality: stacked planes separated by soft, gentle shadows, visible paper fibre and risograph texture, fine halftone grain, a subtle tonal gradient within every plane. Handmade and tactile, never mechanical.

THE BRIDGE IS THE SUBJECT. A single graceful arched bridge spans the entire width of the image: its deck runs off both the left and the right edge of the frame, cropped by them, and its arches carry the composition from edge to edge. The arch rises high enough that the span occupies the vertical middle of the band, leaving no large empty area of sky above it. The bridge is the connective structure everything else hangs from — cut from the same warm paper as both shores, so it belongs to neither side. Small stylised figures walk across it in BOTH directions, some paused mid-span in pairs and small groups, apparently in conversation; slender paper ribbons pass between them, carrying open books one way and softly glowing points of light the other. The exchange is mutual and unhurried. The figures are flat cut-paper silhouettes with no facial features.

The left shore is machine intelligence, rendered softly but unmistakably as a NETWORK: rounded glowing nodes joined by clearly drawn connecting edges, a lattice that spreads and branches across the left of the composition, with smooth ribbons of data drifting in slow arcs between the strands. Organic and luminous in its drawing — curved threads, no hard right angles, no circuit-board traces, no chips, no wiring — but it must read as a network of connections, not as plants, seed heads or flowers.

The right shore is human learning and civic life: a low courtyard of study with open arcades, trees in layered paper foliage, a cluster of small figures seated and standing around tables, open books, stacked pages lifting into the air. Warm, inhabited, human-scaled — a place of teaching, not a monument of state.

Across the middle the two vocabularies gradually exchange properties: paper arcades loosen into curving threads, drifting data ribbons settle into the bridge's stonework, pages become nodes and nodes become pages. Optimistic, luminous, gently futuristic — near-future and hopeful, not industrial.

Light: soft, diffuse, warm daylight from the upper right, with an airy glow lifting off the bridge deck. Shadows stay soft and warm, never harsh — but the image is NOT flat: it holds a clear light-to-dark structure, with real depth in the recesses between the paper layers.

Palette — the grounds are NEUTRAL, not tinted. Bone, cream, warm grey, stone, taupe and a deep warm charcoal, with a full tonal range from lightest to darkest, and that darkest value genuinely present across the whole width of the image rather than in one corner. Distribute the dark tones evenly through the composition; no single dark rectangular block or hard-edged background panel. These neutrals must read as GREYS that lean warm — NOT as tan, beige, orange or sepia. Do not cast the whole image in one hue.

Restrained colour is welcome and the image should not look monochrome: muted, dusty, chalky secondary colours may appear — soft sage and olive green in the foliage, pale ochre, dusty rose-clay, and a cool dove grey, barely blue, in the far distance and in the deepest shadows. Every one of them stays low in saturation, like dyed paper that has been left in the sun.

A single clear lapis blue (#416bcc) is the ONE fully saturated colour in the image, and it must be visibly the most vivid thing in the frame by a wide margin — the only element a viewer would call "colour" rather than "tone". Everything else sits far below it in saturation, so the blue carries the eye on its own. Use it as the thread of connection: a clean continuous line running along the bridge's span from edge to edge, plus a few of the ribbons passing between the figures, and nowhere else. Keep it minimal and precise — a bright line, not a mass. A true mid lapis blue, clearly blue and slightly warm: not navy, not teal, not cyan, not violet, not powder blue.

Because the accent is itself a blue, nothing else in the image may compete with it as a blue. The cool notes in the distance and in the shadows must stay several steps lower in chroma and read as grey that merely leans cool, never as a second blue; the glowing nodes of the network stay warm white and pale, not blue-white. The lapis line is the only saturated blue in the frame, and the warm neutrals around it are what make it carry.

No text, letters, numbers or logos anywhere in the image.

Final output: a 2400 × 600 pixel horizontal banner, 4:1, landscape.
